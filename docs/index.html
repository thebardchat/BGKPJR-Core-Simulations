01 · The Problem

Why Space Is Still Expensive

Current launch systems are fundamentally constrained by the Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation. Every kilogram of payload requires 20–50 kg of propellant just to overcome Earth's gravity well and atmosphere. This inefficiency has kept orbital access expensive and fragile for 70 years.

85–95%
Propellant Mass Fraction
Of a conventional rocket's mass at launch is propellant. Payload is the rounding error.
$10K/kg
Current LEO Access Cost
$2,000–$10,000 per kilogram depending on vehicle and orbit. Reusability has helped — barely.
20:1
Fuel-to-Payload Mass Ratio
For every 1 kg of payload, you lift 20–50 kg of fuel. The Tsiolkovsky Equation doesn't negotiate.
9,400 m/s
Required Δv to LEO
Every meter per second must come from onboard propellant — unless you start accelerating before launch.
Δv = Isp × g₀ × ln(minitial / mfinal) // To reach LEO: Δv ≈ 9,400 m/s · mass ratio ≈ 20:1 · propellant fraction ≈ 95% // The equation is immutable. The only escape: provide Δv before ignition.
02 · The Solution

The Gryphon spacecraft uses aerodynamic lift to climb through the atmosphere. The Kepler solar sail handles deep-space transit at zero propellant cost.

03 · Patent IP

VacuumGate: The LH₂ Membrane

The single hardest physics problem in the BGKPJR architecture is the atmospheric wall at tube exit: a vehicle moving at Mach 5 transitions from near-vacuum to full atmospheric density in milliseconds. The VacuumGate LH₂ cryogenic seal is our patented solution.

VacuumGate · LH₂ Cryogenic Exit Seal
Liquid hydrogen membrane · −253 °C (20 K) · Triple-function architecture
A
Pressure gradient maintenance. The LH₂ membrane seals the pressure differential between near-vacuum tube interior (10⁻³ atm) and full atmospheric exterior, allowing continuous operation without structural sealing mechanisms that would block vehicle egress.
B
Thermal heat sink. During final acceleration the vehicle skin reaches 1,500–2,000 K stagnation temperature. LH₂ at 20K absorbs an extraordinary quantity of thermal energy as it vaporizes, providing active cooling of the vehicle nose during the critical exit phase.
C
Controlled detonation thrust. When the vehicle breaches the membrane, LH₂ vapor instantly mixes with atmospheric O₂. Onboard rockets ignite simultaneously. The engineering challenge: can the detonation wave be controlled to propagate behind the vehicle, not around it?
"This event is either a catastrophic failure or a controlled thrust-boost. The entire engineering problem of BGKPJR-VG reduces to: can we make the detonation wave propagate behind the vehicle, in a controlled direction, at a controlled time? If yes → patent-worthy breakthrough. If no → vehicle and tunnel are destroyed on every launch."

— BGKPJR VacuumGate Feasibility Report, April 2026

Seven Hard Barriers — Documented in Full
  • 1
    Track-Length / G-Force Paradox CRITICAL
    At 4g, 37 km reaches Mach 5 — not orbital velocity. System MUST be hybrid: tunnel provides partial Δv, onboard rockets complete the insertion. Revised target: Mach 5 exit + 35–45% fuel savings. Significant, but not magic.
  • 2
    Vacuum Tube Structural Problem CRITICAL
    1.1 × 10¹⁰ N crushing force on 10m diameter × 37 km tube. A 1 cm² leak admits 30 kg of air per minute. 50–150 MW continuous pumping. Hyperloop hasn't solved this at 600m scale. Mitigation: partial vacuum (10⁻³ atm), not deep vacuum; segmented pumping.
  • 3
    The Atmospheric Wall CRITICAL
    Dynamic pressure q ≈ 1.77 MPa (256 psi) at Mach 5 sea-level exit. Vehicle transitions vacuum to full atmospheric density in milliseconds. Without mitigation: structural destruction. This is the exact problem the LH₂ membrane solves.
  • 4
    LH₂ Seal Chemistry — Controlled Hindenburg SEVERE
    LH₂ at −253°C breached by Mach-5 vehicle: instant vaporization (70 kg/m³ → 1000× volume), O₂ mixing, stoichiometric mixture, guaranteed ignition from plasma-hot skin. The engineering question: detonation behind the vehicle or around it? Patent IP lives here.
  • 5
    Thermal Wall — 1,500–2,000 K at Mach 5 SEVERE
    Mach 5 at atmospheric density produces stagnation temps far beyond shuttle tile rating. Requires active transpiration cooling (sweating skin), magnetohydrodynamic boundary layer control, or the LH₂ membrane absorbing stagnation heat during transit.
  • 6
    Solar Sail Timing RESOLVED IN V3
    Solar sails generate micronewtons of thrust. They cannot lift a vehicle from sub-orbital trajectory — gravity wins every time. Kepler sail is a Phase 4 system: deploys ONLY after stable orbit for deep-space operations. Not a launch system.
  • 7
    Economic Scale — $85–120 Billion CRITICAL
    No single nation or private company can fund this. Comparable: StarTram cargo-only ~$20B; human-rated ~$60B+. BGKPJR-VG with LH₂ infrastructure: $85–120B. Multi-agency world-leader coalition is not optional — it is the only path to construction.
07 · Control Systems

Guidance, Navigation & Control

BGKPJR operates in two fundamentally different flight regimes — each requiring a distinct control architecture. Full implementations are in /control_systems.

LQR
Linear Quadratic Regulator
PHASE · Atmospheric Flight
Optimal linear state-feedback controller for the Gryphon spacecraft during atmospheric ascent. Minimizes a quadratic cost function over state and control effort. Designed for the Mach 3.5 → Mach 8+ climb profile with nonlinear aerodynamic effects linearized around multiple operating points along the trajectory.
MPC
Model Predictive Control
PHASE · Maglev Track Acceleration
Receding-horizon optimization for maglev track trajectory. Computes optimal coil switching sequences and magnetic field profiles to maintain vehicle centerline, minimize lateral oscillation, and hit exit velocity targets within structural G-load limits. Runs at kHz update rates during 28.7 km acceleration phase.
ATT
Attitude Stabilization
PHASE · All Flight Phases
Six-degree-of-freedom attitude control across all mission phases: tube exit transition shock, hypersonic climb, exo-atmospheric coast, orbital insertion, and Gryphon return glide. Reaction control system + aerodynamic surfaces for phase-appropriate authority.
