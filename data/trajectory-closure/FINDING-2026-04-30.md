# Trajectory Closure Simulation — First Real Finding (2026-04-30)

## Summary

The trajectory closure simulation `simulation/src/trajectory_closure.py` ran for the first time on 2026-04-30. It produced a substantive engineering finding that affects the canonical architecture:

**The "pod self-circularizes to LEO from rail exit" architecture (as drafted in the 2026-04-30 reconciliation) DOES NOT CLOSE under any combination of pod 2nd-stage propellant load and rail inclination within the BGKPJR-001 patent envelope (Mach 3-5, 15-45° inclination, ≤5G).**

The earlier `tug-pro` architecture (pod boosts to high suborbital, Tug catches at apogee, Tug provides circularization Δv) is the architecture that closes. My reconciliation that changed pods from "ballistic + Tug-catch-at-apogee" to "pod-self-LEO + Tug-only-TLI" was an architectural error caught by the simulation before reaching Lukens.

At 4500 kg propellant on a 4200 kg pod (>100 % propellant fraction — physically impossible), apogee is 344 km. Single-stage pod self-circularization to LEO **does not close** within the patent envelope.

## What Closes

The trajectories that DO reach high apogee (200–344 km) are perfect catch points for a Space Tug. The original `tug-pro` architecture had this right:

1. Pod exits rail at canonical Mach 5
2. Pod 2nd-stage burn pushes apogee to ~200 km
3. **Tug catches at apogee** (sub-orbital)
4. Tug provides circularization Δv (~5–6 km/s) to LEO
5. Tug provides TLI Δv (~3.15 km/s) to lunar orbit

This requires a Tug with ~9 km/s outbound Δv budget — significantly more than the canonical 4.5 km/s budget. The Tug grows substantially.

OR alternatively:

1. Pod uses two stages (jettisonable first stage) — much higher mass ratio
2. Pod self-circularizes to LEO with two-stage architecture
3. Tug provides only TLI (~3.15 km/s) — small Tug

Either architecture is patent-compliant. The trade study deliverable is which one wins on cost-per-kg.
