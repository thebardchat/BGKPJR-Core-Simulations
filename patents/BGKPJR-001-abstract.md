## Abstract

A multi-stage space launch system comprising:

1. A terrestrial magnetic levitation linear accelerator track inclined at 15 to 45 degrees from horizontal, housed within a partially evacuated tube structure maintaining pressure between 0.05 and 0.2 atmospheres;

2. A hypersonic lifting-body spacecraft ("Gryphon") featuring variable geometry wings that remain retracted during tube acceleration to maximize bore efficiency and deploy upon exit to generate aerodynamic lift, converting ballistic trajectory into a lifting ascent;

3. An orbital solar sail propulsion module ("Kepler") for station-keeping and orbital maneuvering, deployed only at stable orbit above 300 km altitude.

The system utilizes ground-based electromagnetic forces to accelerate the payload to velocities exceeding Mach 3 (1,020 m/s) prior to atmospheric interaction, significantly reducing the onboard chemical propellant mass fraction required for orbital insertion as defined by the Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation. The initial delta-v provided by the maglev system reduces the traditional 9,400 m/s orbital insertion requirement by approximately 15-20%, enabling corresponding reductions in propellant mass and increases in payload fraction.

## Claims

### Claim 1 (Independent - System)
A launch system wherein the initial velocity (Delta-V) is provided by ground-based electromagnetic forces rather than onboard chemical combustion, comprising:
- A superconducting maglev track utilizing niobium-titanium (NbTi) or similar superconducting materials;
- An evacuated tube structure maintaining partial vacuum conditions;
- Linear synchronous motor propulsion achieving exit velocities between Mach 3 and Mach 5;
- Human-tolerable acceleration profiles not exceeding 5g sustained.

### Claim 2 (Dependent - Variable Geometry)
The system of Claim 1, further comprising a spacecraft with variable geometry wings that:
- Remain retracted during tube acceleration to maximize bore efficiency and minimize aerodynamic interference;
- Deploy upon tube exit within 5 seconds to generate aerodynamic lift;
- Convert ballistic trajectory into a lifting ascent, improving effective mass-to-orbit ratio.

### Claim 3 (Dependent - Thermal Protection)
The system of Claim 1, wherein the spacecraft includes an active transpiration cooling system for the nose cone region, comprising:
- A porous outer surface through which coolant is injected;
- A coolant delivery system providing flow rates between 0.1 and 2.0 kg/s;
- Temperature management maintaining surface temperatures below material limits during hypersonic flight.

### Claim 4 (Dependent - Solar Sail)
The system of Claim 1, further comprising an orbital propulsion module utilizing solar radiation pressure, wherein:
- The solar sail comprises thin-film polyimide material less than 10 microns thick;
- The sail area exceeds 500 square meters;
- The module is deployed only at altitudes exceeding 300 km to avoid atmospheric drag.

### Claim 5 (Independent - Method)
A method for launching a spacecraft to orbit comprising:
1. Positioning the spacecraft within an evacuated launch tube;
2. Activating superconducting electromagnets to levitate the spacecraft;
3. Accelerating the spacecraft using linear synchronous motor propulsion;
4. Releasing the spacecraft from the tube at hypersonic velocity;
5. Deploying aerodynamic surfaces upon tube exit;
6. Continuing ascent using combined aerodynamic lift and rocket propulsion;
7. Achieving orbital velocity using onboard propulsion;
8. Optionally deploying solar sail for orbital operations.

### Claim 6 (Dependent - Acceleration Profile)
The method of Claim 5, wherein the acceleration profile comprises:
- A ramp-up phase increasing acceleration from 0 to maximum over 1-2 km;
- A constant acceleration phase at 3-5g for the majority of the track;
- A ramp-down phase reducing acceleration to 2-3g before tube exit.

### Claim 7 (Independent - Track Structure)
A launch track structure comprising:
- A tube structure of length exceeding 15 km and diameter exceeding 5 m;
- Vacuum pumping systems maintaining pressure below 0.2 atmospheres;
- Superconducting magnet assemblies for levitation and propulsion;
- Cryogenic cooling systems maintaining magnet temperature below 10 K;
- An inclined installation at angles between 15 and 45 degrees from horizontal.

### Claim 8 (Dependent - Energy Storage)
The system of Claim 7, further comprising an energy storage system capable of:
- Storing at least 50 GJ of energy;
- Delivering peak power exceeding 1 GW for at least 30 seconds;
- Utilizing flywheel, supercapacitor, or battery energy storage technologies.

## Prior Art Distinction

This invention is distinguished from prior art including:

1. **StarTram (Powell/Danby):** Our system uses an inclined track rather than a horizontal track with vertical exit ramp, reducing structural complexity and enabling variable exit angles.

2. **Space Gun concepts:** Our system uses magnetic levitation eliminating barrel wear and enabling precise velocity control, and operates at human-tolerable g-loads.

3. **Traditional rockets:** Our system provides significant initial delta-v from ground-based infrastructure, reducing propellant requirements.

4. **Aircraft launch (SpaceShipTwo):** Our system achieves significantly higher release velocities (Mach 3.5+ vs. Mach 0.8), providing greater delta-v advantage.
